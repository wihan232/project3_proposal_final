History and Origin of the Ad-Hominem Fallacy

Significant Historical Figures

Aristotle

Records of ad-hominem being explicitly mentioned have dated back as early as Ancient Greek periods with Aristotle laying out the groundwork for the concept of ad-hominem being a personal attack towards someone rather than refuting the argument itself. Aristotle was among the first scholars to deem the ad-hominem to be fallicious in the pejorative sense in his work, Sophistical Refutations.

Locke

Known for his writing in philosophical concepts, such as his book, Of the Conduct of the Understanding, and many more, Locke contributed to the definition of the ad-hominem fallacy. Locke was credited as coining the ad arguments ( ad hominem, ad verecundiam, ad ignorantiam, and ad judicium). He was the first to use the term argumentum ad hominem in his work An Essay Concerning Human Understanding.
